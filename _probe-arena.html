How the pairing works
01
One material input set per theme

A board theme declares only --b-a, --b-b, --b-line, --b-coord and --b-plate. Cells, inlays, coordinates, light-mode pastels and every state colour are derived from those five values.

02
Light mode is a derivation, not a second theme

In light mode each cell is mixed toward warm chalk with color-mix(), so a theme is never authored twice and can never drift out of sync.

03
Warm boards remap the states

Copper, sandstone and ironstone carry their own selection, move and threat colours, because amber-on-copper would fail contrast. The rest inherit the accent untouched.

04
Shipped pairing

Forged Steel (TEX01) on Brushed Graphite (T01) is the default: 15.4:1 body text in dark, 16.6:1 in light, and the lowest glare of the twenty materials.

The Forged system

Cold steel and one forge amber in dark; warm chalk and burnished bronze in light. Twenty backgrounds, twenty board materials, a single accent that always means “this is the action the game is waiting on”.

Complete rules

How Fortress is played

Two moves, then one simultaneous exchange of fire. Walls are structure, not strength. Nothing is captured — everything is destroyed, including what destroyed it.

Turn Board Pieces Support Combat Promotion Endings Board reading

The turn

A turn is four steps, in this order, every time:

1Move one piece. Pick any piece of your colour and a highlighted destination. A piece that has moved may not move again this turn — the one exception is the Keep, which may step twice, but never straight back to the hex it came from.
2Move a second piece. If nothing can legally move, the game goes straight to step 3.
3Preview. Incoming damage appears as a −N badge on every piece in contact, along with what would die. Nothing has happened yet — you may undo either move.
4Accept. Combat resolves simultaneously and the turn passes to the other player.
Exception
White's first turn is a single move.

White opens with one move, then Accept. Every later turn — for both players — is two moves.

The board

63 pointy-top hexes in nine rows, widening from 6 to 8 and back: 6 · 7 · 8 · 7 · 8 · 7 · 8 · 7 · 6. Each side has one Keep, one Turret, two Bastions, two Towers and ten Walls — sixteen pieces each.

Direction
White pushes east, black pushes west.

Every hex has a rank, 2q + r. White's rank rises as it advances, black's falls. The far right edge (rank 6) is white's target; the far left (rank −8) is black's.

Notation
Files a–i, rows counted from the bottom.

a1 is the bottom-left hex and i6 the top-right. A sub-move reads like Wc4–e5: piece letter, from, to.

Pieces

Strength is never stored — it is recalculated from core value plus support every time it matters. The number printed on each plate is that total, right now.

PieceCoreMovement
Keep K3One hex step in any of the six directions. May move twice in a turn, but never straight back to where it started.
Tower T2Slides any distance along one of the six directions, stopping when blocked. It cannot jump.
Bastion B2Steps one hex in any direction, or vaults over a contiguous run of pieces that begins adjacent and contains at least one friend, landing on the first empty hex beyond it. It may vault over enemies.
Turret U1Jumps to any empty hex at distance exactly two — over anything in between, friends included.
Wall W0Steps one hex forward along either forward diagonal, or straight ahead as an advance. On its first move it may slide two hexes along a forward line. It never moves backwards or sideways.
A wall's strength comes entirely from support — and an unsupported wall collapses at the next Accept.

Support

Support is given by friends, never by the piece itself, and only in a straight line distance sense: adjacent, or exactly two hexes away. Only the Keep supports at distance two.

Example
A Tower beside a wall is worth 3, not 2.

Tower core 2 + one adjacent friendly wall (+1) = STR 3. Add a friendly Keep at distance two and it becomes 4. Support is the whole game: mass beats spread.

Combat

Combat is simultaneous. Every piece deals its current strength to every adjacent enemy at the same instant — so a piece that is about to die still lands its blow, and a piece that kills its attacker may fall to that attacker's damage anyway.

1Each piece's incoming damage is the sum of the current strength of every adjacent enemy.
2A piece is destroyed if incoming damage is greater than or equal to its strength.
3All destroyed pieces are removed at once. A wall reduced to strength 0 — including by the loss of the friend that was supporting it — collapses, and that collapse can take its neighbours with it.
4If a Keep is gone, the game is over. If both Keeps go in the same combat, it is a draw.
The wall trap
An unsupported wall always dies.

A wall has core strength 0. With no friendly neighbour its strength is 0, so at every Accept it collapses — whether or not anybody attacked it. The board draws it with a dashed amber outline so you can see it coming before you commit.

Promotion

Each side has three promotion hexes on its far edge — the extreme corner hexes of the enemy back ranks (rank 6 for white, rank −8 for black). They are drawn on the board with a dashed gold outline whenever it is your turn.

A wall that lands on one of them never stays a wall: it is promoted immediately, and you choose what it becomes.

Tower Core 2, slides any distance. The choice when the promotion hex is quiet — it keeps the wall's push and adds reach.
Turret Core 1, jumps exactly two, over anything. The choice when the hex is contested — it can leave again through a closing gap.
Also automatic
A wall already standing on a promotion hex promotes too.

If a wall begins your turn on a promotion hex — shoved there by another piece, or left over from a vault — the game asks you the same question at the end of the turn.

StalemateDrawThe player to move has no legal move at all — rare, because walls and the Keep can almost always find a step.
Threefold repetitionDrawThe same position (pieces, sides, and whose turn) occurs three times.

The adjudication countdown is the meter in the Turn panel: thirty quiet turns, reset by any destruction or any new ground taken.

Reading the board

Every state a piece or a hex can be in has a shape of its own, so the board still makes sense in grayscale, at a glance, on a phone in daylight.

SignalMeans
DotAn empty hex this piece can legally reach. A dot over an enemy piece means the destination is occupied — you can never move onto a piece.
ReticleA dark ring around an enemy piece: it is in contact with something you have selected or moved. A heavy reticle means the exchange is lethal.
−N badgeIncoming damage in the preview: this piece will take N damage when you accept.
Red outlineThe piece is destroyed by the current preview.
Dashed amberA wall with no support: it collapses when the turn is accepted.
Gold ringThe selected piece, or the keyboard cursor. Enter selects; Escape clears.
Faded cellThe origin and destination of the move just played.
Gold dashesA promotion hex for the side to move.

Notation

Sub-moves are written as piece letter, origin, en dash, destination — Wc4–e5. A promotion adds =T or =U, both moves of a turn are comma-separated, and the turn ends with ×N when N pieces were destroyed or collapsed by that Accept.

Letters

K Keep · T Tower · B Bastion · U Turret · W Wall. Because both armies share the letters, the log prefixes each turn with W or B and numbers it, so a whole game can be replayed from the log alone.

A turn, step by step

1You move a wall two hexes on its opener: Wc4–c6.
2You move a Tower up behind it: Tb3–b6. It now supports nothing and threatens nothing — yet.
3The preview shows the wall at c6 with one adjacent friend, so its plate reads 1 — and any enemy in contact carries a −N badge, where N is that enemy's current strength.
4You accept. Damage is dealt both ways at once, and the log records the turn with its destruction count: Wc4–c6, Tb3–b6 ×1.

The whole game — 32 pieces, 63 hexes, simultaneous combat, eight endings — runs on the maths on this page. If the board ever disagrees with this text, the board is wrong; tell the builder.
